Our proposal

Every channel connects directly to SAP

Pros
+
One hop per message
An order crosses a single connector to reach SAP, and the new stock number crosses one to get back. Nothing waits on a relay.
+
SAP stays the source of truth
Inventory is owned in one place and published to every channel at once, so no channel drifts out of step with another.
+
Failure stays local
A connector outage takes one channel offline. The other four keep selling.
+
B2B terms survive the trip
Dentira's GPO contract pricing and PO-style orders land in the system that models them, instead of being flattened into a storefront order.
+
No extra integration
Shopify is one of the five channels, not a hub. The connector count is identical either way.
Cons
−
Five SAP-facing integrations
Each channel is mapped and maintained on its own; there is no shared translation layer to fix things in one place.
−
Load lands on SAP directly
Peak concurrency and API rate limits have to be handled connector by connector.
−
No single console
Seeing all channel traffic in one view takes deliberate monitoring; the topology doesn't hand it to you.

Initial concept

Every channel routes through Shopify to reach SAP

Pros
+
One familiar console
Orders from every channel appear in the Shopify admin the team already works in each day.
+
One SAP-facing contract
Only APPSeConnect touches SAP, so SAP-side change control and certification cover a single interface.
+
A shared normalization point
Product data is cleaned once in Shopify's catalogue rather than mapped channel by channel.
Cons
−
An extra hop on every message
Orders and stock updates both cross Shopify. SAP says 90 while the channels still show 100 — and that gap is the overselling window.
−
One extra integration
Every channel still needs its own connector; they just point at Shopify. Add APPSeConnect and it is six integrations, not five.
−
One outage stops everything
Shopify or APPSeConnect going down takes all channels offline at once, rather than one.
−
A data model that doesn't fit
Shopify has no native B2B contract pricing, GPO terms or PO-style orders, so Dentira's terms have to be mapped into a DTC order object.
−
Storefront reporting drifts
Marketplace and GPO volume lands in Shopify's own numbers, and consumes order volume and API budget that was never a Shopify sale.

Recommendation

Connect every channel directly to SAP

Where we landed
Direct-to-SAP costs the same number of connectors and removes a hop from every order and every stock update. Routing through Shopify adds a sixth integration and a shared point of failure without retiring any of the other five.
What the initial concept gets right
One place to watch orders, and one interface to certify against SAP, are both real needs — and they stay on the table. It is the relay that carries the cost, not the goal behind it.
How we'd meet those needs
Keep Shopify as a channel and put the single console in a connector dashboard reading from SAP — one view of all channel traffic, no extra hop.
If Shopify is still the preferred hub
Then we scope it openly: budget for the sixth integration, a custom mapping for GPO and contract orders, and a reconciliation process that checks both hops.

How to read this board

Same shapes and same spacing in both lanes

Solid blue, downward — an order travelling toward SAP.
Dashed teal, upward — the updated stock number travelling back out to the channels.
Blue outline — SAP, the system of record.
Pink outline — where an option adds risk, with the annotation attached to it.
Equal spacing — every hop is drawn the same distance, so the lane that ends lower is the one that takes longer.
