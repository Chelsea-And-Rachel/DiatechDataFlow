Both shapes cost five connectors, one per platform we know of today, and routing through Shopify does not save one. Four of them just stop at Shopify instead of SAP, and everything then funnels through the fifth.
Congestion
Five channels of orders and five channels of stock updates share one connector, in both directions. That single path is where traffic backs up at peak, and where one failure takes the whole business offline.

Recommended

Every channel connects directly to SAP

Pros
+
One hop per message
An order crosses a single connector to reach SAP, and the new stock number crosses one to get back. Nothing waits on a relay.
+
SAP stays the source of truth
Inventory is owned in one place and published to every channel at once, so no channel drifts out of step with another.
+
No congestion point
Traffic reaches SAP through five independent connectors, one for each platform known today. A spike on one channel never queues behind another, and each path can be tuned or rate limited on its own.
+
Failure stays local
A connector outage takes one channel offline. The other four keep selling.
+
Shopify stays a storefront
Nothing asks Shopify to route other channels' orders, hold their stock or model their pricing. It does the job it was built for, and nothing else.
+
B2B terms survive the trip
Dentira's GPO contract pricing and PO-style orders land in the system that models them, instead of being flattened into a storefront order.
+
No connector does double duty
Shopify is one of the five channels, not a hub. Both shapes cost the same five connectors, one per platform known today, but here each serves a single channel rather than one of them carrying the other four.
+
Each channel mapped on its own terms
Every connector is built for the platform it serves, so one channel's quirks never have to be forced through a shape that suits another. Changing one leaves the other four untouched.
+
The console stays a deliberate choice
Nothing forces the single view into a storefront admin. A connector dashboard reading from SAP gives one view of all channel traffic, built to fit, and without adding a hop.

Client proposed

Every channel routes through Shopify to reach SAP

Cons
−
An extra hop on every message
Orders and stock updates both cross Shopify, so every message travels two hops instead of one. One more place for it to stall, and one more thing to reconcile when it does.
−
Every order is translated twice
A Dentira order is mapped into Shopify's model, then Shopify's model into SAP's. Two translations per message, through a middle model with no GPO terms to hold. Connecting directly maps it once.
−
Everything queues at one connector
At peak, five channels of orders and five channels of stock updates pass through APPSeConnect in both directions. Its rate limits become the whole business's rate limits, and no channel can be given priority without starving another.
−
Only one path ever touches SAP
SAP-side certification and change control cover a single interface, which sounds simpler. It also means all five channels sit behind that one path, so any change, fix or throttle on it reaches every channel at once.
−
Shopify asked to work as a mini ERP
Order routing, inventory master, channel fan-out and B2B pricing are ERP jobs. This shape asks a storefront platform to do all of them, and Shopify was never built for that.
−
One outage stops everything
Shopify or APPSeConnect going down takes all five channels offline at once, rather than one.
−
A data model that doesn't fit
Shopify has no native B2B contract pricing, GPO terms or PO-style orders, so Dentira's terms have to be mapped into a standard storefront order object.
−
Channel-only products still sit in Shopify
A product meant only for Net32 or a GPO still has to exist in Shopify's catalogue for the relay to carry it, even though it should never appear on the storefront. Each one has to be hidden deliberately, and one wrong flag puts it on sale.
−
Storefront reporting drifts
Marketplace and GPO volume lands in Shopify's own numbers, and consumes order volume and API budget that was never a Shopify sale.

Recommendation

Connect every channel directly to SAP

Where we landed
Both shapes cost the same five connectors, one per platform known today, so the choice is not about how many get built. It is about how they are wired.
Connecting every channel straight to SAP takes a hop and a translation out of every order and every stock update, keeps one channel's outage away from the other four, and leaves no single connector carrying the whole business. Routing through Shopify saves nothing on build and adds a shared path that all five channels have to queue behind, in a platform that was never meant to hold inventory or price a GPO contract.
How we'd meet those needs
The goals behind the client proposed shape are real, and not one of them needs the relay to happen.
One place to watch orders becomes a connector dashboard reading live from SAP, with every channel in a single view and no extra hop. One interface to certify becomes a shared connector framework, built once and reused per channel, so SAP-side change control stays manageable across all five. Clean product data is handled at source in SAP and the PIM, so it is already right before it reaches any channel, and channel-only items never have to sit in a storefront catalogue. Shopify keeps doing what it is best at, selling as one of the five channels.
