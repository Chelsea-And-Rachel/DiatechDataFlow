Our proposal

Every channel connects directly to SAP

Pros
+
One hop per message
An order crosses a single connector to reach SAP, and the new stock number crosses one to get back. Nothing waits on a relay.
+
SAP stays the source of truth
Inventory is owned in one place and published to every channel at once, so no channel drifts out of step with another.
+
Failure stays local
A connector outage takes one channel offline. The other four keep selling.
+
B2B terms survive the trip
Dentira's GPO contract pricing and PO-style orders land in the system that models them, instead of being flattened into a storefront order.
+
No connector does double duty
Shopify is one of the five channels, not a hub. Both shapes cost the same five connectors — one per platform known today — but here each serves a single channel rather than one of them carrying the other four.
+
Each channel mapped on its own terms
Every connector is built for the platform it serves, so one channel's quirks never have to be forced through a shape that suits another. Changing one leaves the other four untouched.
+
Load spreads across five paths
Traffic reaches SAP through five independent connectors, one for each platform known today, so peak volume on one channel never queues behind another's — and each can be tuned or rate-limited on its own.
+
The console stays a deliberate choice
Nothing forces the single view into a storefront admin. A connector dashboard reading from SAP gives one view of all channel traffic — built to fit, and without adding a hop.

Initial concept

Every channel routes through Shopify to reach SAP

Pros
+
One SAP-facing contract
Only APPSeConnect touches SAP, so SAP-side change control and certification cover a single interface.
+
A shared normalization point
Product data is cleaned once in Shopify's catalogue rather than mapped channel by channel.
Cons
−
An extra hop on every message
Orders and stock updates both cross Shopify, so every message travels two hops instead of one — one more place for it to stall, and one more thing to reconcile when it does.
−
Every order is translated twice
A Dentira order is mapped into Shopify's model, then Shopify's model into SAP's — two translations per message, through a middle model with no GPO terms to hold. Direct-to-SAP maps it once.
−
One outage stops everything
Shopify or APPSeConnect going down takes all channels offline at once, rather than one.
−
A data model that doesn't fit
Shopify has no native B2B contract pricing, GPO terms or PO-style orders, so Dentira's terms have to be mapped into a standard storefront order object.
−
Channel-only products still sit in Shopify
A product meant only for Net32 or a GPO still has to exist in Shopify's catalogue for the relay to carry it — even though it should never appear on the storefront. Each one has to be hidden deliberately, and one wrong flag puts it on sale.
−
Storefront reporting drifts
Marketplace and GPO volume lands in Shopify's own numbers, and consumes order volume and API budget that was never a Shopify sale.

Recommendation

Connect every channel directly to SAP

Where we landed
Both shapes cost the same five connectors — one per platform known today — so the choice isn't about how many get built — it's about how they're wired. Direct-to-SAP removes a hop and a translation from every order and every stock update; the relay saves neither, and puts all five channels behind one path.
What the initial concept gets right
One interface to certify against SAP, and one place to clean product data, are both real needs — and they stay on the table. It is the relay that carries the cost, not the goals behind it.
How we'd meet those needs
Keep Shopify as a channel, clean the catalogue at source, and put the single view in a connector dashboard reading from SAP — one place to watch every channel, no extra hop.
If Shopify is still the preferred hub
Then we scope it openly: budget for the second translation layer, a custom mapping for GPO and contract orders, and a reconciliation process that checks both hops.
